Tenure Report 
​
Introduction 
Boundless Travel Society at IITM BS is a vibrant and diverse community that connects over 
5200+ members, including 1200+ female members and 110+ core members. Our mission is 
to organize memorable group travel experiences and cultural events that bring together 
individuals with a shared passion for exploration and travel. Through these initiatives, we 
strive to create a community of like-minded individuals who can build lasting friendships, 
exchange cultural insights, and foster a deeper understanding of the world around us. 
Our mission is to: 
●​ Promote cultural exchange and educational opportunities through travel. 
●​ Encourage exploration of domestic and international destinations. 
●​ Organize city meetups, themed trips, and collaborations with other societies. 
●​ Foster personal development, networking, and meaningful connections. 
●​ Grow as a community that blends fun with learning through travel. 
The term has been highly successful, with numerous trips, meetups, and collaborations 
across 40+ cities, uniting people to celebrate travel and adventure. This report highlights 
the events and activities organized by the society, showcasing their impact and 
engagement.

1.1 Tricolor Trails 2.0  
(Independence Week Multi-City Series) 
 
Series Summary 
To celebrate the spirit of Independence Week, Boundless launched Tricolor 
Trails 2.0, a nationwide multi-city initiative conducted across 12 cities in 11 
states. Held in collaboration with Nallamala and Sundarbans, the series aimed 
to bring BS students together through cultural exploration, bonding, and 
patriotic celebration. 
From lakes, wildlife sanctuaries, heritage museums, temples, and malls to 
waterfalls, caves, and iconic city parks  each meetup reflected the unique 
character of its location. Across cities like Patna, Nagpur, Bhubaneswar, 
Mumbai, Delhi, Bangalore, Kolkata, Gorakhpur, Jamshedpur, Indore, Jaipur, 
and Chennai, students engaged in introductions, games, local visits, café 
hangouts, and proudly sang the National Anthem as part of the Tricolor Trails 
movement. 
The series successfully connected students nationwide, creating shared 
memories of friendship, unity, and cultural appreciation.

Summary 
This three-day expedition through the heart of Karnataka offers a perfect 
blend of nature, adventure, culture, and spiritualism. The journey takes 
travelers from the lush green landscapes and wildlife of Shimoga to the 
majestic peaks of the Western Ghats and concludes along the serene and 
beautiful coastline of Udupi. This trip is designed to create a memorable 
experience, combining trekking, wildlife encounters, spiritual visits, and 
coastal adventures. 
Day 1: Wildlife, Poetry, and History 
After an early breakfast, the journey begins with a visit to the Sakrebyle 
Elephant Camp, offering a unique opportunity to see elephants up close in 
their natural habitat. The trip then proceeds to Kavimane in Kuppalli, the 
ancestral home of the great Kannada poet Kuvempu. After soaking in the 
literary and cultural heritage, the group will have lunch at Kavishaila, a rock 
monument dedicated to the poet. The day concludes with an adventurous trek 
to the historic Nagara Fort, where participants can explore the ruins and 
witness a breathtaking sunset over the backwaters.

The second day is dedicated to adventure in the Western Ghats. It starts with

Mewar Trip 
Event Overview 
This report documents the successful Boundless Travel Society trip to the 
historic region of Mewar, encompassing Udaipur, Mount Abu, and Chittorgarh, 
which took place over four eventful days. The trip, themed "Padharo Mhare 
Desh: History, Lakes, and Hills," provided a rich blend of culture, history, and 
natural beauty, ensuring a truly unforgettable experience for all participants. 
Day 1: The Grand Arrival in Udaipur 
The expedition commenced with our grand arrival in Udaipur, the 
magnificent 'City of Lakes,' where we immediately immersed ourselves in 
Rajput history. Our first major stop was the majestic City Palace, where we 
spent well over two hours exploring its intricate courtyards, marble balconies, 
and the fascinating crystal gallery, marking a truly grand start to the trip. This 
was quickly followed by a brief, spiritual visit to the nearby, beautiful Jagadish 
Temple. For lunch, we enjoyed a stunning affair, securing a spot overlooking 
the lake, with many opting for the exquisite views at Ambrai or Upre. The 
entire afternoon was defined by the calming Lake Pichola boat ride, which 
transported us right up to the famous Jag Mandir Island. The evening 
delivered the essential cultural highlight: catching the folk dance and puppet 
show right on time at 7 PM at Bagore Ki Haweli. We capped the night with

fortunate enough to catch a beautiful sunset here, just as planned. The day 
wound down after souvenir shopping in the local markets, followed by a 
shared, delicious group dinner and some fun group games before calling it a 
night.

Day 4: Chittorgarh - History's Grand Finale 
The final day culminated in a powerful reminder of Rajput bravery and history 
at Chittorgarh. The entire morning and afternoon were dedicated to exploring 
the massive Chittorgarh Fort, a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the 
largest in India. We thoroughly explored its sheer scale and the historical 
significance of its structures, including the triumphant Vijay Stambh, the 
revered Kirti Stambh, and the symbolic Padmini Palace. Local Rajasthani

Some trips are planned.​
Some trips are remembered. 
And then there are journeys like Meghalaya - the kind that quietly stay with 
you long after the bags are unpacked. 
Over four days, winding roads carried us through misty hills, roaring 
waterfalls, silent forests, and villages that felt untouched by time. Somewhere 
between the rain and the clouds, strangers slowly turned into friends, and 
every moment began to feel a little more boundless. 
 
Day 1 - From Lakes to Canyons 
The adventure began at Guwahati Railway Station, where sleepy travellers 
slowly gathered with backpacks, excitement, and a hint of curiosity about the 
days ahead. The journey into Meghalaya started with a scenic drive toward 
Shillong.

Our first stop was the serene Umiam Lake, a place where time seems to slow 
down. The calm water stretched endlessly, reflecting the surrounding hills and 
the pale morning sky. Breakfast by the lakeside felt peaceful, almost 
meditative - a perfect welcome to the land of clouds. 
Soon after, we arrived in Shillong, often called the Scotland of the East. At 
Ward’s Lake, gentle walking paths curved around quiet waters, and blooming 
cherry blossoms added a splash of colour to the misty surroundings. 
The afternoon took us higher into the hills to Shillong Peak, where the entire 
city unfolded beneath us like a miniature landscape. From there, we continued 
to Laitlum Canyon, where dramatic cliffs opened into vast valleys that seemed 
to disappear into the clouds. 
Standing there, watching fog drift through the canyon, it was easy to 
understand why Meghalaya feels almost magical. 
By evening, the journey continued toward Sohra (Cherrapunji). After a long 
but beautiful day, dinner and conversations slowly filled the night, and the 
first friendships of the trip began to form. 
 
 
Day 2 - Where Waterfalls Sing 
Morning in Sohra arrived with cool air and drifting mist - the perfect setting for a day 
dedicated to waterfalls. 
Our first stop was the iconic Nohkalikai Falls, one of the tallest plunge 
waterfalls in India. From the viewpoint, the water plunged dramatically into a 
turquoise basin far below, surrounded by towering cliffs and lush greenery. 
It was the kind of view that makes everyone go silent for a moment. 
Next came the trek to Wei Sawdong Falls, a hidden multi-tier waterfall tucked 
deep inside the forest. The path down was steep and adventurous, but the 
 
74 
 

=== PAGE 75 (images: 1) ===
reward was breathtaking - crystal pools and cascading streams flowing over 
rock terraces. 
After lunch in Sohra, the adventure continued underground at the Mawsmai 
or Arwah Caves, where narrow passages, limestone formations, and dim 
natural light created a mysterious world beneath the hills. 
By evening, the group gathered at Seven Sisters Falls, where multiple streams 
flowed down a massive cliff face like ribbons of water across the 
mountainside. 
The night ended back in Sohra with dinner, laughter, and stories from the 
day’s adventures - each person slowly becoming part of a shared journey. 
 
 
Day 3 - Roots, Rivers & Villages 
If the first two days showcased Meghalaya’s dramatic landscapes, the third 
day revealed its soul. 
The morning drive through the Khasi Hills was filled with winding roads, 
drifting clouds, and endless greenery. 
Soon we arrived at Dawki, home to the famous Umngot River - a river so clear 
that boats appear to float in mid-air. Gliding across the crystal water felt 
almost surreal, as stones and riverbeds were visible far beneath the surface. 
After lunch near the river, we walked toward Riwai’s Living Root Bridge - a 
remarkable natural bridge formed by guiding the roots of rubber trees across 
streams over many years. Built through patience and tradition, these bridges 
are living symbols of harmony between people and nature. 
Later in the evening, we visited Mawlynnong, known as Asia’s cleanest village. 
Neatly maintained pathways, bamboo houses, and smiling locals created a 
calm and welcoming atmosphere.

There was something simple yet powerful about the place - a reminder that 
beauty often lies in the way people care for their surroundings. 
Dinner and overnight stay in Dawki wrapped up another unforgettable day. 
 
Day 4 - The Final Cascade 
The final day began quietly, with breakfast and a visit to Phe Phe Falls. 
Hidden deep within Meghalaya’s landscapes, the waterfall flowed gracefully 
into a peaceful pool surrounded by cliffs and forest. It was the perfect place to 
pause and reflect on the journey. 
Some people sat quietly by the water.​
Others took photos or shared stories from the trip. 
It felt like everyone was trying to hold onto the moment just a little longer. 
After a relaxed morning, the group enjoyed a picnic-style lunch before 
beginning the return journey to Guwahati. 
As the bus rolled through the hills one last time, conversations softened into 
quiet smiles and sleepy reflections. 
By evening, the trip concluded at Guwahati Railway Station, marking the end 
of the journey - but certainly not the end of the memories.
